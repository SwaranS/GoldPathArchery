## How to use this document

This is the current product decision record. **Confirmed** means the product owner has supplied the direction. **Partial** means the direction is agreed but behaviour remains unresolved. **Proposed** means a recommendation awaiting review. **Deferred** means a decision will intentionally be made later.

Details recorded as proposed are not approved requirements. Finish this document before updating the remaining requirements and architecture documents.

## Established direction

Build a fresh archery training service for coaches and athletes, supporting recurve, compound and barebow scoring at launch. Support juniors in the private pilot. The first release covers accounts, athlete profiles, personalised training plans generated by a human System Plan Manager using AI and software, with athlete/parent/coach editing and sharing, multiple coaching relationships, weekly plans, session logging, basic progress summaries and administration.

Club, state and team memberships are future scope. Payments, competition registration, equipment sales, video analysis, social feeds and native apps remain outside the first release. AI-assisted plan generation under a human System Plan Manager is included in the first-release direction, superseding the original exclusion of automatic AI coaching for this specific capability; other autonomous coaching features are not assumed. A reusable template library is not required in version one.

## D02 — Human System Plan Manager with AI-assisted generation

Confirmed product direction:
- System Plan Managers are humans who use AI and software to generate personalised shooting plans. Multiple people may hold this role.
- The default plan duration is 12 weeks.
- Users with plan-editing permission can move an individual planned session or exercise to another day without swapping with an activity on that day.
- Moving a session leaves other sessions on both days unchanged; entire-day schedule swapping is not required.
- Users with plan-editing permission may move a session onto a planned Rest Day. Show a warning and require confirmation before applying the move.
- After confirmation, the destination displays the moved session and is no longer presented as a rest-only day; preserve the original rest-day designation in schedule-change history.
- Athlete/parent/coach age and permission rules apply to moving sessions; view-only users cannot move sessions.
- Plan duration is configurable for shorter or longer periods, up to a maximum of one year.
- Weekly AI recommendations and Plan Manager review continue throughout the selected plan duration.
- Plan Managers are involved at both stages: reviewing and publishing initial plans, and reviewing weekly AI change recommendations before releasing them to the athlete/parent/authorised coach.
- AI takes the lead in drafting under the System Plan Manager's direction; the role is not an autonomous AI agent.
- Athletes aged 16+ may edit their own plans.
- Parents managing junior profiles and authorised coaches may edit the athlete's plan within their agreed permissions.
- Ages 14–15 remain view-only; this decision does not expand junior editing rights.
- Plan Manager review is part of both stages, rather than an exception-only workflow. The athlete's own coach is not required to approve every plan unless separately agreed.
- Releasing reviewed weekly recommendations does not apply them automatically: eligible-user acceptance is still required. Plan Manager status alone does not grant authority to accept changes on an athlete's behalf.
- Plan Managers can access only athletes explicitly assigned to them; the role does not grant access to all athletes.
- Another Plan Manager gains access only through explicit reassignment or a temporary cover assignment.
- Temporary cover access ends when the cover assignment ends; reassignment removes access based on the former assignment.
- Assignment-scoped access does not grant score-editing rights or authority to accept weekly changes on the athlete's behalf. Exact accessible data fields and assignment-administration permissions remain to be defined.
- This restores the human System Plan Manager role and supersedes the earlier wording that removed that role.
- Sharing creates a separate, independent copy owned by the receiving archer.
- Changes to either plan do not alter the other, and the sender gains no access to the recipient's data through sharing.
- The recipient's copy follows the existing age and access rules: ages 14–15 remain view-only, with an eligible parent or authorised coach managing edits.
- Only plan instructions and structure are copied; the sender's profile, logs, scores and feedback are not shared.
- AI reviews completed training and available feedback weekly and recommends adjustments to upcoming sessions.
- Weekly recommendations require acceptance by an eligible athlete aged 16+, a parent managing the junior profile, or a coach with the necessary plan-editing permission before changing the active plan.
- Ages 14–15 can view recommendations but cannot accept changes.
- Until accepted, the active plan remains unchanged. Completed-session history is preserved.
- Eligible users can accept, edit or reject each weekly AI recommendation individually.
- Preserve a recommendation decision history containing the original suggestion, its affected sessions, the user's decision, any edited version, the acting user and decision time.
- Record which accepted or edited recommendation was applied and the resulting plan version; rejected recommendations remain recorded without changing the active plan.
- Recommendation history follows existing athlete/parent/coach/Plan Manager access rules and the account-closure deletion/anonymisation policy; keeping history does not grant new access or indefinite identifiable-data retention.

Proposed session-move behaviour:
- Select an individual planned session or exercise and a destination date, then confirm the move.
- Move its instructions and planned targets together without changing recorded scores or completed-session history.
- Do not exchange or overwrite other sessions on either date.
- Preserve a record of the schedule change and use the revised schedule in subsequent AI recommendations.
- Warn about conflicts with recorded range availability, competitions or recovery constraints.
- Before implementation, confirm handling of moves outside the plan period and partially completed activities. Moves onto Rest Days with warning/confirmation are agreed.

Proposed weekly review details:
- Consider actual versus planned arrow volume, session completion, comparable scores, recorded effort, training notes and known schedule or competition changes where available.
- Explain each proposed change and its supporting observations; show current and proposed sessions together.
- Treat missing logs as unknown rather than assuming the athlete did not train.
- Preserve human edits and highlight any proposed changes to edited sessions.
- Review the current plan version when applying accepted changes so newer human edits are not silently overwritten.
- If evidence is insufficient, explain the uncertainty and recommend retaining the plan or requesting missing information rather than inventing progress.
- Weekly review timing remains to be defined. Individual acceptance, editing and rejection with decision history are confirmed above.

Proposed MVP behaviour:
- Generate a personal plan from the athlete's goals, experience, current training volume, schedule, range access and competition dates.
- Ask for missing essential inputs rather than inventing athlete details.
- Validate generated plans against agreed scheduling, volume and progression rules before making them active. Define these rules and evaluate plan quality before pilot use.
- Show the plan's assumptions and allow eligible users to edit it.
- Save plan versions and identify whether changes came from AI, the athlete, a parent or a coach.
- Preserve completed-session history. AI regeneration must not silently overwrite human edits; propose changes for review or preserve edited sessions.
- If generation or validation fails, retain the last usable plan and explain what needs attention.
- Include AI usage costs when evaluating the EUR 45 monthly testing budget.

Still to decide:
- Who can create, change and end Plan Manager assignments, and how are cover durations set?
- Who can change plan duration, and how should a duration change affect existing future sessions? The 12-week default and one-year maximum are confirmed.
- Which athlete data fields can an assigned or covering Plan Manager access? Assignment-only access is confirmed.
- Which AI provider or external service will generate plans? No provider or integration is selected.
- What day/time should weekly recommendations be prepared, and can users request an additional review?
- What is the detailed scope of coach plan-editing permission, and how are conflicting edits handled? Per-coach view versus edit selection is confirmed.
- How is a shared copy received and activated? Copy ownership is confirmed; receipt and activation flow remain to be defined.

AI-assisted generation under the human System Plan Manager is in MVP scope. The other project documents will be reconciled only after this product decision document is completed.

## D04/D05 — Coaching access and listings

Confirmed product direction:
- Multiple coaches and mutual invitations are required.
- Optional profile listings are visible only to signed-in users.
- User profiles include a "Stay out of Search" toggle. When selected, the user is excluded from user searches and discovery listings.
- Under-16 athlete profiles are always excluded from searches/listings; this setting cannot make them discoverable.
- Athletes aged 16+ and coaches may choose whether to appear in listings. "Stay out of Search" is off by default for eligible new accounts, so they are discoverable by signed-in users unless they enable it. Under-16 athlete profiles remain excluded regardless of the default.
- Search visibility does not grant access to private training data or change existing coaching relationships and permissions.
- All invitation links are private and intended for the invited recipient.
- Hidden users control new connections: only they can initiate an invitation involving their hidden profile; other users cannot search for or initiate invitations to that hidden profile.
- For an under-16 athlete, the managing parent initiates invitations on the athlete's behalf.
- Acceptance by the intended recipient is required before a connection grants access.
- Opening or forwarding a link alone does not grant access or make a hidden profile discoverable.
- Existing accepted connections remain governed by their granted permissions when a user enables "Stay out of Search".

- An athlete aged 16+ chooses each connected coach's plan access. For athletes under 16, the parent managing the junior profile makes that choice.
- Connected coaches have view-only plan access by default. Editing requires an explicit grant for that athlete.
- Permission is set separately for each coach and can be revoked without ending the coaching relationship.
- A view-only coach cannot modify the plan or apply AI recommendations. A coach with plan-editing permission may do so within the agreed editing scope.
- Ages 14–15 cannot grant or revoke coach permissions.
- These permissions do not grant authority over another athlete or another coach's private notes.
- From age 16, the athlete chooses each linked parent's continued access: view-only, editing or no access.
- Ending a coaching relationship immediately revokes the coach's access to the athlete's active plans, logs, scores, feedback and historical records.
- The coach can still access a separate contribution history containing their own proposals and authored revisions. This is not access to the athlete's full historical plan or later activity.
- The contribution record must exclude unrelated athlete data and later changes by the athlete, parent, AI or other coaches.
- The athlete retains access to the coach's original contributions in their own training history after the relationship ends, subject to existing athlete/parent access and account-closure rules.

Proposed contribution-history details:
- Preserve a dated record of each coach-authored proposal or revision, identifying its author and the content contributed.
- Historical proposal records are read-only; they cannot be used to edit or reopen the former athlete's plan.
- Show proposal status only as captured while access was active; do not expose subsequent athlete actions after termination.
- Retain only the coach's contributed instructions rather than copying the athlete's full plan, logs or private context.
- Apply D12 deletion/anonymisation rules to contributions too. Coach authorship does not exempt athlete-related personal information from account-closure processing.
- Retention periods, identity labels, content redaction and exact proposal-history fields remain to be agreed.

Proposed MVP behaviour:
- Access starts only after an invitation is accepted by the eligible account holder or guardian.
- Being labelled a physio or technical coach alone grants no additional access. Detailed access to profile fields, logs and other records remains to be defined separately from plan access.
- Coaches do not automatically gain access to another coach's private notes.
- Either eligible party may end a relationship; the immediate revocation and separate contribution-history rules above apply.
- A personal copy of a plan already shared is distinct from continuing access to the originating athlete's data.
- Define invitation expiry, revocation and intended-recipient verification. Private links and hidden-user initiation are confirmed above.
- A parent handles coaching invitations for a junior whose access is view-only.

Confirm the proposed rules above before treating them as requirements; the confirmed per-coach plan-access model is already agreed.

## D06 — Junior profiles, logins and transitions

Confirmed product direction:
- Under 14: the parent manages a child athlete profile; no independent child login.
- Ages 14–15: a junior login can view that athlete's assigned plans, session history and progress.
- From 16: the athlete can edit their own training data, including recording sessions and adapting personal plans within the agreed athlete permissions. This does not grant coach, plan-manager or administrator authority.
- At ages 14–15, view-only means no changes to training data, no logging, no plan sharing and no management of coach relationships.
- Until the age-16 transition, the parent continues to enter logs and make permitted changes. Coaches may act only within their separately agreed permissions.
- Reaching 14 does not automatically remove parental access.
- From age 16, the athlete chooses whether a linked parent has view-only access, editing access or no access.
- The athlete can subsequently change or revoke that parent access.
- View-only parent access permits no changes to plans, logs or scores. Parent editing access includes score entry/corrections within the agreed athlete/parent permissions.
- Parent status alone does not override the athlete's age-16 access decision.

Proposed design implication: retain one stable athlete profile and link the junior's login to it, rather than duplicating or moving training records. Account security and privacy requests need separate handling from read-only training permissions.

Still to decide:
- How is the confirmed age-16 editing transition activated and verified?
- How are age and guardian authority verified, and how is the junior login activated?
- How is the parent-access choice presented at the age-16 transition, and what access applies while awaiting the athlete's choice? The athlete's right to choose viewing/editing/no access is confirmed.

A read-only login is a product permission, not a substitute for a lawful basis for processing children's data. In Ireland, the digital age of consent is 16 where an online service relies on consent. The proposed age-14 viewing threshold does not by itself establish independent consent.

Reference: [Irish DPC guidance on children's data and parental consent](https://www.dataprotection.ie/sites/default/files/uploads/2023-04/DPC_ChildrensData_ParentalConsent.pdf).

## D11 — Arrow-by-arrow scoring

Confirmed product direction:
- Indoor and outdoor scoring are essential.
- Scoring supports recurve, compound and barebow at launch. Round and score validation must account for the selected bow type.
- Indoor scoring supports 30-arrow rounds, 60-arrow rounds and double 60-arrow rounds (two 60-arrow rounds, 120 arrows overall). Distances, target faces and bow-specific score rules remain to be confirmed.
- Outdoor scoring supports 720 and double 720 rounds.
- Any unfinished round can be saved and resumed later, preserving the entered scores and the next entry position.
- Saved incomplete rounds remain clearly distinguishable from completed rounds.
- Adding future scoring formats, such as 1440 and 900 rounds, must be straightforward. These are future extension examples, not additional launch formats.
- A training score containing only 36 arrows is valid and can be saved without completing the full outdoor round.
- Store and display the actual number of scored arrows and the corresponding maximum score. A saved 36-arrow training result is distinguished from a completed 720 or double 720.
- Unscored arrows are not treated as misses or zeros; comparisons must account for the different arrow counts.
- When scoring a round, users must be able to record each arrow's individual score.
- Arrow-by-arrow entry is essential first-release functionality.
- Launch scoring supports both numeric keypad entry and plotting arrow positions by tapping the target face.
- Both entry methods record the same underlying arrow scores; physical arrow identifiers remain optional.
- An optional physical arrow identifier, such as shaft #7, may be associated with each scored shot. This is what the product owner means by arrow number; it is distinct from shot order.
- Physical arrow identifiers are selected only from the athlete's saved arrows; free-text/manual identifier entry is not available while scoring.
- Users can save arrows in named sets, such as indoor and outdoor sets.
- An athlete profile can hold multiple saved bows.
- Each saved bow can store sight marks for different distances, with distance units recorded, for lookup at the range.
- Sight marks are optional and apply to bows using a sight; barebow equipment does not require them.
- Users can select which saved bow and arrow set they use for a session or round, including using bow X one day and bow Y the next.
- Selecting equipment for a new session or round must not change the equipment recorded against earlier activity.
- Selecting an arrow remains optional. Users without saved arrows can still score a round without physical identifiers.
- Users can score a complete round without entering physical arrow identifiers. An omitted identifier does not mean a missing score or a miss.
- A totals-only approach does not meet this requirement.
- Score entry and correction are controlled by the athlete or managing parent: athletes aged 16+ can enter and edit their own scores; a parent manages scoring for an under-16 athlete.
- Ages 14–15 may view scores but cannot enter or edit them.
- Coaches cannot enter or edit the athlete's scores, including coaches with plan-editing permission.
- Plan Manager status does not grant score-entry or correction rights. Viewing scores remains subject to separately agreed access.
- AI recommendations cannot change recorded scores.
- The eligible athlete or managing parent can correct an incorrectly entered score after a round is completed.
- Preserve a correction history showing the original and corrected values, who made the correction and when.
- Recalculate affected end, round and session totals after a score correction; coach and Plan Manager score-editing restrictions still apply.
- From age 16, a parent can enter or correct scores only if the athlete has granted that parent editing access; viewing or no access does not allow score edits.

Proposed MVP behaviour:
- Organise a round into numbered ends with a configured number of arrows per end.
- Proposed interaction: allow users to switch between keypad and plotting without duplicating shots. Numeric-only entries have no assumed target coordinates; define boundary scoring and correction behaviour for plotted shots.
- Track shot sequence (end number and position within the end) independently of any physical arrow identifier.
- Calculate end subtotals and running round totals from individual scores.
- Distinguish unrecorded arrows from misses; individual-score corrections and post-completion correction history are confirmed above.
- Validate each score, including X and miss where supported, against the selected scoring format.
- Preserve arrow-level data for progress analysis and weekly AI recommendations.
- Proposed design: define round formats through reusable, versioned scoring definitions covering stages/distances, target faces, ends, arrows per end, allowed scores and bow-specific rules.
- Support format definitions with multiple stages or distances so future 1440/900 formats do not require redesigning the core scoring flow.
- Preserve the scoring definition used by an existing round when format definitions change; historical and resumed rounds must remain consistent.
- Confirm the rules for each new format before enabling it. The exact format-management workflow will be defined during architecture/design.

Still to decide:
- Which details are required for saved bows and arrows, and how are they edited or retired while preserving historical equipment records? Named arrow sets, multiple bows and selection of equipment per session/round are confirmed.
- Which distances, target faces, arrows per end and bow-specific scoring rules apply to the confirmed indoor/outdoor formats?
- Indoor double 60-arrow and outdoor double 720 rounds are confirmed. Which other combinations of rounds within a session are required?
- Is analysis by physical arrow identifier required in MVP or later? Numeric entry, target-face plotting and optional identifier capture are confirmed for launch.
- How should a user mark a partial training round as finished without completing its full scheduled arrow count? Saving and resuming any unfinished round is confirmed.
- Define the correction interaction for plotted shots so target position and recorded score remain consistent. Post-completion athlete/parent score corrections with history are confirmed.

The earlier proposed totals-only MVP approach is superseded. The scoring interaction should be reviewed before implementation.

## D12 — Account closure and anonymisation

Confirmed intent: attempt to anonymise all user-related data on leaving/deleting an account, while preserving useful data only where it is genuinely anonymous.

Proposed operating rules:
1. Treat leaving as an explicit closure request; sign-out, inactivity or ending a coaching relationship alone does not trigger deletion.
2. Offer a data export before closure and verify that the requester is authorised. Junior and guardian closure rules require explicit definition.
3. Revoke account access, invitations and active relationships when closure is confirmed.
4. Assess all relevant records, including profiles, session histories, free text, relationships, identifiers, logs and any files.
5. Do not treat changing a name to "Anonymous" as sufficient. Remove or generalise identifying details and linkages; prefer anonymous aggregate statistics where detailed histories could identify someone.
6. Delete data that cannot be adequately anonymised, subject to any documented retention obligation.
7. Define closure-processing deadlines and finite backup retention. Restoring a backup must not permanently reintroduce deleted accounts or data.
8. Keep any required retained records restricted, purpose-limited and subject to an agreed expiry.

Still to decide: export format, processing deadline, backup expiry, inactive-account policy and what anonymous statistics have a useful purpose. No guarantee is made that every detailed record can safely be retained.

References:
- [Irish DPC: storage limitation and retention](https://dataprotection.ie/en/faqs/responsibilities-data-controllers/how-long-should-personal-data-be-held-meet-obligations-imposed-gdpr)
- [Irish DPC: anonymisation and pseudonymisation](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/anonymisation-pseudonymisation)

## D14 — Proposed pilot defaults

These are review suggestions, not commitments:
- Pilot: 5–10 athlete profiles, at least 2 coaches and at least 1 parent-managed junior.
- Observe usage for 4 weeks.
- At least 80% of pilot participants complete their role's core journey without assistance.
- At least 60% of athlete profiles have weekly recorded activity in 3 of the 4 weeks, including parent-entered activity.
- No unresolved critical access-control or data-loss defects at release.
- Collect feedback from each participating role.

## D15 — Session feedback

Confirmed product direction:
- Sessions support completed, partially completed and skipped status.
- Record actual arrows shot for shooting sessions separately from planned arrows.
- Perceived effort is optional, on a 1–10 scale.
- Training notes are optional.
- Scores are linked through the scoring feature rather than a second independent score field.
- Session feedback supports weekly AI recommendations and Plan Manager review within the agreed access rules.
- Plans include basic non-shooting sessions, such as strength, mobility and mental practice, at launch.
- Non-shooting sessions use a simple activity title/type, brief instructions, completion status and the existing optional effort/notes fields. No arrow count is required.
- Keep non-shooting details basic in MVP; detailed exercise prescriptions, sets/repetitions, weights and specialised tracking are future extensions.
- Planned rest days appear on the calendar explicitly marked "Rest Day" and require no logging.
- Rest days are not treated as skipped or incomplete training sessions.

Proposed details still to settle:
- An unlogged session remains unknown rather than automatically becoming skipped.
- Define labels for the effort scale so users apply it consistently.
- Preserve the latest actual training information without modifying the originally planned targets.
- No dedicated diagnosis, treatment or injury-record fields are assumed in MVP. Free-text notes still require appropriate access restrictions.
